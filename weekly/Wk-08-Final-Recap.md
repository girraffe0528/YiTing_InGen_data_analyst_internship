# Week 8 Final Recap

Week 8 focused on bringing the analytical work from the internship together into a complete Physical AI data analytics capstone. The main deliverable was the capstone report, which integrated the earlier work on telemetry EDA, classification modeling, feature importance, model robustness, time-series forecasting, anomaly detection, hypothesis testing, and dashboard development. Organizing these analyses into one report helped me see how the individual methods connect as parts of a larger fleet analytics workflow rather than as separate technical exercises.

One of the most important findings was that strong model performance still requires careful interpretation. Random Forest achieved a Macro-F1 of 0.9327 for Aido Rover operational-state classification, but FAULT remained the most difficult class and class weighting did not improve its recall. The synthetic Sentinel Prime AI transfer experiment also showed that strong performance on one platform does not guarantee that the same model will transfer successfully to another platform. This reinforced the importance of platform-specific validation.

The time-series and anomaly-detection analyses provided another important perspective. Holt exponential smoothing achieved an RMSE of 0.1076 for fleet-average battery SoC forecasting, while anomaly-detection performance varied considerably depending on the fault mechanism. Statistical detection performed particularly well for controlled faults with explicit sensor signatures, while Isolation Forest provided a more general multivariate approach but generated more false positives.

The final Aido Rover Fleet Analytics Dashboard helped connect these results to an operational context by combining fleet status, classification insights, battery monitoring, and anomaly alerts in one interface. Developing the dashboard and capstone report also reinforced the importance of communicating analytical findings clearly rather than presenting model outputs without interpretation.

Overall, Week 8 demonstrated how my previous experience in environmental science, transportation data analysis, and data visualization can transfer to Physical AI analytics. The methods are transferable across domains, but their interpretation must remain specific to the system being analyzed. This internship has strengthened my understanding of how exploratory analysis, predictive modeling, temporal analysis, anomaly detection, statistical testing, and visualization can work together to support data-driven monitoring of Physical AI systems.
