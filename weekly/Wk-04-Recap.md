# Week 4 Recap
This week, I focused on evaluating the robustness of the Aido Rover operational mode classification models developed in Week 3. The main goal was to determine whether the strong Random Forest performance remained stable across different training subsets, how class imbalance affected FAULT detection, and whether the model could generalize to another InGen platform.

Random Forest was the most stable model. In five-fold stratified cross-validation, it achieved a mean Macro-F1 of **0.9307 with a standard deviation of only 0.0008**. Logistic Regression achieved a considerably lower mean Macro-F1 of **0.7376 ± 0.0023**. The consistently high Random Forest scores across all five folds indicate that its performance was not dependent on a particular train-test split. This provides stronger evidence that Random Forest is the more reliable model for classifying Aido Rover operational modes.

The feature that best distinguished FAULT from PATROL was **`wheel_imbalance`**, which measures the difference between the highest and lowest wheel torque values at each observation. In the FAULT-versus-PATROL permutation analysis, disrupting this feature produced the largest decrease in FAULT F1-score. The front-wheel torque measurements were also among the strongest predictors. Operationally, this suggests that differences in wheel behavior may provide useful information for detecting abnormal operating conditions, although this relationship would need to be validated using real Rover telemetry because the current dataset is synthetic.

Class imbalance remained an important limitation. FAULT represented only about **4.9%** of the training data, and the baseline Random Forest achieved a FAULT recall of **0.7858**. Class weighting did not improve this result; FAULT recall decreased to **0.7680**. Most missed FAULT observations were classified as PATROL, suggesting that imbalance alone may not explain the remaining classification errors.

Finally, I tested cross-platform transfer using synthetic Sentinel Prime AI telemetry. Directly applying the Aido-trained model reduced accuracy from **0.9662 to 0.7084** and Macro-F1 from **0.9298 to 0.6387**. This experiment suggests that the modeling framework may be reusable across platforms, but platform-specific training, validation, or adaptation is likely necessary when telemetry patterns differ.
