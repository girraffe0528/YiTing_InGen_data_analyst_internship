# Week 1 Recap

During the first week of my internship, I explored InGen Dynamics' AI ecosystem and learned how different platforms generate and utilize data to support intelligent decision-making. As I became more familiar with each platform, I found it helpful to compare their data structures with datasets that I had previously worked with in environmental science and data analytics. This comparison allowed me to connect my previous experience with the new concepts introduced during the internship.

Among all the InGen platforms, Rover most closely resembles the atmospheric science datasets that I worked with as a Research Assistant at National Taiwan University. My previous research involved analyzing precipitation, sea surface temperature, and atmospheric observations collected continuously over time to understand climate variability and the impacts of different El Niño patterns. Similarly, Rover continuously generates telemetry data from multiple sensors, including GPS, cameras, LiDAR, and system health information. Although the applications are different, both datasets consist of continuous sensor observations that are suitable for time-series analysis, trend identification, anomaly detection, and performance monitoring. This similarity made Rover the most familiar platform for me because the overall analytical workflow closely matches the methods I previously applied in climate research.

In contrast, Sentinel most closely resembles the U.S. Traffic Accident Severity dataset that I analyzed during my data analytics project. The traffic accident dataset contained event-based records where each observation represented an individual accident with associated information such as weather conditions, location, time, and severity. Likewise, Sentinel records security-related events by combining sensor observations with AI-generated classifications and alerts. Both datasets emphasize event detection and classification rather than continuous monitoring. In my previous project, I developed classification models to predict accident severity, while Sentinel applies similar analytical concepts to distinguish normal activities from potential security threats. Although the domains differ, both require structured event records that support classification, pattern recognition, and data-driven decision-making.

This comparison helped me realize that the transition from environmental science to Physical AI is more natural than I initially expected. While the data sources and application domains have changed, the core analytical methods remain highly transferable. My previous experience with time-series analysis, classification, and data visualization provides a solid foundation for understanding InGen's AI platforms. I look forward to applying these analytical skills throughout the internship while continuing to learn more about Physical AI and intelligent automation.
