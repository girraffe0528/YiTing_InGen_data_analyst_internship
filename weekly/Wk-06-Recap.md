# Week 6 Recap

This week focused on anomaly detection and statistical hypothesis testing using Aido Rover telemetry. The anomaly detection analysis compared a statistical threshold approach with Isolation Forest across five controlled fault types: motor stall, accelerated battery degradation, GPS disruption, LiDAR saturation, and IMU axis failure. The comparison was repeated across ten random seeds to evaluate whether the observed performance was consistent across different fault injection locations.

The statistical threshold approach performed particularly well for faults with clear and interpretable sensor signatures. GPS disruption, LiDAR saturation, and IMU axis failure were detected reliably because these faults produced explicit changes such as missing GPS coordinates, LiDAR readings reaching the sensor limit, or an IMU axis becoming zero. This resembles environmental anomaly detection, where physically meaningful thresholds can identify extreme observations when the expected range of a variable is well understood.

Motor stall and accelerated battery degradation were more difficult because their signatures depended on changes over time rather than a single abnormal sensor value. The statistical approach used rolling baselines to detect unusual wheel-torque behavior and Battery SoC changes. Across the ten-seed evaluation, it achieved higher mean AUROC than Isolation Forest for all five fault types. However, Isolation Forest produced higher recall for motor stall and battery degradation, showing that the unsupervised model could identify some abnormal observations missed by the threshold method.

Isolation Forest was less effective for faults that were not strongly separated in the raw feature space. GPS missingness and IMU zero-axis failures were especially difficult because the model was trained primarily on raw telemetry values rather than explicit temporal or sensor-state rules. This highlights an important difference between general unsupervised anomaly detection and domain-informed statistical monitoring.

The comparison connects closely with the anomaly methodology I previously used in environmental science. Similar to detecting extreme climate events, statistical thresholds are effective when anomalies can be defined relative to a meaningful baseline. However, Physical AI telemetry includes multiple interacting sensors and temporal behaviors, making a hybrid approach more practical. Explicit statistical or rule-based thresholds can monitor known fault signatures, while unsupervised models such as Isolation Forest can provide complementary detection for less clearly defined anomalies.

The hypothesis tests also reinforced the importance of separating statistical significance from operational importance. Battery SoC and LiDAR saturation showed statistically significant relationships with FAULT behavior, but both effect sizes were very small. This suggests that reliable rover monitoring should combine multiple telemetry signals rather than relying on a single sensor measurement.
